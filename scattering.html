EXPERIMENT 05 / NUMERICAL QUANTUM MECHANICS

Quantum scattering & tunnelling

Propagate a complex Gaussian wavefunction through a free region, a rectangular barrier or a potential well. The finite-difference Crank–Nicolson integrator is unitary to solver precision with a real, static potential and Dirichlet boundary conditions.

━━ Probability density |ψ|² ━━ Potential V(x), scaled

Coordinate domain x = −30…30; fixed zero-value wavefunction at both walls. The right/left readouts measure probability outside the central region, not asymptotic transmission/reflection coefficients. Stop before the packet reaches a wall.

Model and limitations

i∂ₜψ = [−(1/2m)∂ₓ² + V(x)]ψ, with ℏ = m = 1. The tridiagonal Crank–Nicolson scheme advances an explicitly complex state using a centered discrete Laplacian.

Wavepacket tunnelling is indicated by amplitude on the far side of a barrier; a complete transmission measurement requires separating outgoing packets before boundary reflections. The optional quartic complex absorbing potential absorbs outgoing waves near the walls; lost norm is accounted for separately. It is not perfectly reflectionless, and relativistic effects are not modeled. Discretization error remains even when norm is conserved.
