What the picture means

Each ring compresses one three-dimensional snapshot of the modelled lab. A and B occupy fixed local positions (−d/2, 0, 0) and (d/2, 0, 0). The dot locations on a ring are labels, not their physical coordinates. Circle area encodes probability; the selected pair is enlarged. Line width encodes concurrence, from 0 (unentangled) to 1 (maximally entangled).

T₀ is an arbitrary reference time. Changing a control recalculates the history from the chosen initial state. It does not apply a pulse midway through the experiment.

Try three experiments

  1. Exchange: start with A excited, J = 1 and no dephasing. At local time 0.785 T₀ the pair is nearly maximally entangled. At 1.571 T₀ the excitation has moved to B.
  2. Same probabilities, different state: compare the two 50/50 initial states. The wall probabilities match, while the entanglement link differs. Add dephasing to the entangled state and scrub time.
  3. Different clocks: choose SR and set speed to 0.60, or choose GR and set compactness to 0.36. In either case, the local clock advances at 80% of the comparison clock. Equal local elapsed times give equal quantum states.
The mathematics, in brief

The pair has one excitation, shared between states |10⟩ and |01⟩. In the isolated case, starting with A excited:

∣ψ(τ)⟩=cos⁡(Jτ)∣10⟩−isin⁡(Jτ)∣01⟩.|\psi(\tau)\rangle=\cos(J\tau)|10\rangle-i\sin(J\tau)|01\rangle.
LaTeX source
|\psi(\tau)\rangle=\cos(J\tau)|10\rangle-i\sin(J\tau)|01\rangle.

The general calculation uses a density matrix and a Lindblad dephasing equation. It is solved analytically, with no integration step error. Local elapsed time is τ = a·t, where a = 1 locally, √(1 − v²/c²) in the SR comparison, or √(1 − 2GM/rc²) for the static GR comparison.

The tunnel history records spatial geometry, quantum state and a chosen time label. Nonspatial state variables supply extra descriptive dimensions; they do not add directions in physical space. See MODEL.md for the equations, assumptions and broader SM/GR architecture.

Physical scope and limits

This is a small nonrelativistic quantum lab with an effective exchange interaction. It is not a simulation of the whole universe, the Standard Model, or quantum gravity.

The SR mode compares a small inertial lab with another frame. The GR mode treats a small lab held stationary outside a nonrotating spherical source, with negligible tidal variation and backreaction. It uses an established clock rate on a prescribed background; it does not evolve gravity.

The interior line is a correlation visualization, not a physical wire or signal channel. Both the visible wall data and the information represented inside are quantum. There is no universal present or extra spatial interior in this construction.
