EXPERIMENT 02 / COSMIC EXPANSION

The tunnel can grow.
And sometimes contract.

Follow a universe whose geometry evolves with its contents. Matter, radiation, curvature and a cosmological constant determine the history. Light stretches or compresses as it travels through it.

An evolving universe

↔ Drag across the tunnel to choose a slice. The slider also works with arrow keys.

Energy-density shares at this slice

Space changes from one slice to the next

Ring radius is proportional to the scale factor a(t). Dots follow fixed comoving positions: their proper separation grows with a(t). The drawing fits each history to the available space; its round cross section does not specify the actual shape or total size of the universe.

The sliders specify densities relative to a reference critical density at a = 1. Curvature follows Ωₖ = 1 − Ωₘ − Ωᵣ − ΩΛ. It contributes to the expansion equation but is not an energy-density share in the bar. Matter includes ordinary matter and cold dark matter as one pressureless component.

Experiments to try

  1. Change the ingredients. Compare matter-only, radiation-only and cosmological-constant-only histories. Their expansion follows different time dependences.
  2. Find the turnaround. Choose the closed recollapsing universe. Scrub past the widest point: expansion becomes contraction. The late contraction can blueshift a photon above its original emission energy.
  3. Track an emitted photon. The photon is emitted at the first displayed slice. Its wavelength scales with a; its energy scales as 1/a. The wave uses a fixed illustrative distance scale within each display.
  4. Watch the density shares change. Radiation thins as a⁻⁴, matter as a⁻³, while cosmological-constant density stays fixed. Equal components at one slice need not be equal later.

\left(\frac{H}{H_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^2=\frac{\Omega_r}{a^4}+\frac{\Omega_m}{a^3}+\frac{\Omega_k}{a^2}+\Omega_\Lambda.

H = (da/dt)/a is the expansion rate. The time slider uses u = Href(t − tstart). This is elapsed cosmic proper time for comoving observers, measured in reference expansion times. No particular measured Hubble constant is assumed.

The engine evolves the acceleration equation, so it can pass smoothly through H = 0 into contraction. The first slice is a = 0.2. The history stops at a = 3, a = 0.05 during contraction, or u = 8. These are display limits, not physical ends of time.

χ is the accumulated comoving light-path length in units c/Href since emission. It is not the full particle horizon. On a closed spatial geometry it tracks path length rather than the shortest distance to the starting point.

Photon wavelength λ/λemit = a/astart; energy E/Eemit = astart/a, using E = hc/λ. An ideal freely redshifting blackbody radiation bath similarly has T/Tstart = astart/a. The engine exports this temperature ratio when radiation is present.

How the layers connect

The contents affect geometry through the Friedmann equations. Geometry determines dilution and redshift. A test photon travels through that evolving geometry and has a quantum energy E = hf. The earlier two-qubit lab remains available as a separate controlled experiment; its coupling is not automatically changed by cosmic expansion.

The background is homogeneous and isotropic. It captures the large-scale average rather than galaxies, bound atoms, star formation or a full quantum field calculation. Cosmic time belongs to this cosmological matter frame. It does not impose a universal simultaneity on all observers.

The defaults are illustrative rounded parameters, not a fitted cosmological dataset. A pure cosmological-constant scenario is exponential expansion, but is not an implementation of an inflaton or reheating.
