What the lines actually mean

The packet solves the free-particle Schrödinger equation, not a numerical approximation. Its position probability density is Gaussian:

|ψ(x,t)|² = exp(−(x−μ(t))²/(2σ(t)²)) / (√(2π) σ(t))

μ(t) = x₀ + (ℏ k₀/m)t and σ(t) = σ₀ √(1 + (ℏ t/(2mσ₀²))²); ℏ is set to one in the implementation.

The initial condition is ψ(x,0) = (2πσ₀²)^(-1/4) exp[−(x−x₀)²/(4σ₀²) + ik₀x] up to a physically irrelevant global phase. The displayed complex-amplitude curves are computed from an analytically evolved phase.

Important: the plotted density integrates to one over the full real line; a finite viewport may omit tails. The graph illustrates an ideal single nonrelativistic particle in free space, with no potential, measurement or interactions. It is not yet quantum field theory or a model of expanding spacetime.
