Three directions to move in. A history to travel through. And far more information than a picture of space can show. This is a playful way to explore how geometry, matter and quantum states fit into one description.

The question that started it

Instead of adding more spatial directions, could we add more ways of describing a state? Position, time, fields, particles, amplitudes, probabilities and relationships all tell us something different.

Imagine stacking snapshots along a tunnel. The wall carries quantities we choose to display. Correlations can span the picture’s interior. Move to another slice and see what the laws of physics changed.

Give the picture rules

A metaphor becomes an experiment when it has specified variables, an evolution law and a result we can check. Each room in this lab pairs the tunnel view with a small, well-defined physical model.

The tunnel’s shape is a drawing convention. Its centre is not an extra place where quantum mechanics lives: the wall data and the correlations are parts of the same quantum description.

A starting point, not a final destination

The larger ambition is to connect space, time, matter and interactions in an understandable framework. The Standard Model, general relativity and quantum mechanics already supply much of that vocabulary. Our experiments implement selected pieces and leave clearly named places for the rest.

The field notes connect the idea to those theories. The roadmap describes how to add more physics without losing track of what each calculation means.
