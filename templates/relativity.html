EXPERIMENT 03 / SLICING SPACETIME

Whose slice
is “now”?

A tunnel suggests a stack of moments. Relativity adds a twist: observers in relative motion use different stacks. Drag the teal handle to change the observer’s speed, or the purple handle to choose their time slice.

Teal handle: horizontal drag changes speed. Purple handle: vertical drag changes the time slice. Both have sliders.

T = ct/L and X = x/L. The scale L is arbitrary. The two other spatial axes are suppressed. This is a spacetime diagram, not a view of objects inside a room.

Two flashes. Two definitions of simultaneous.

Events A and B are fixed at (T, X) = (0, −1) and (0, +1). They are simultaneous in the original frame, shown by the horizontal dashed line. At β = 0.60, the moving observer assigns them T′A = +0.75 and T′B = −0.75. These events are spacelike separated, so their order can depend on the frame without reversing a causal influence.

The orange diagonals are light rays through the origin. The teal line is the moving observer’s worldline, X = βT. The purple line contains events simultaneous at the selected T′. Moving the handle changes a coordinate description; it does not send a signal or change either event.

\[T'=\Gamma(T-\beta X),\qquad X'=\Gamma(X-\beta T),\qquad\Gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}.\]

Solving the first equation for the original time gives the purple slice:

\[T=\beta X+\frac{T'}{\Gamma}.\]

Every boost preserves the spacetime interval:

\[-T'^2+X'^2=-T^2+X^2.\]

What this changes about the tunnel

We can keep a chosen sequence of slices as a useful description. We just should not treat that sequence as a uniquely preferred universal present. The cosmology experiment has a particularly useful cosmic time associated with its comoving matter; that does not make all moving observers agree on simultaneity.

The quantum lab’s SR clock setting uses the same proper-time relationship along a single small lab’s worldline. It does not attempt to Lorentz-transform a spatially extended interacting quantum apparatus.
